Network Efficiency Metrics

🔬 What This Analysis Shows:

Network efficiency measures how well information flows through the social network. Global Efficiency shows the average efficiency of information transfer between all pairs of users. Small World Coefficient indicates if the network has "small world" properties (high clustering + short paths). Robustness measures how well the network resists attacks. Power Law R² shows if the network follows a scale-free structure (few highly connected users, many with few connections).

Statistical Significance Tests

🧪 What These Tests Reveal:

Normality Tests (Shapiro-Wilk): Determine if centrality measures follow normal distributions. Most social networks show non-normal distributions (p < 0.05), indicating complex social structures.

Correlation Analysis: Shows relationships between different centrality measures. High correlations (r > 0.5) suggest similar influence patterns, while low correlations indicate diverse influence types.

Statistical Significance: p-values < 0.05 indicate statistically significant relationships, meaning the patterns we observe are not due to random chance.

Comparative Benchmarking

📊 What This Benchmarking Shows:

vs Random Network: Compares our Facebook network to a randomly connected network with same size. Real social networks typically have higher clustering and different path lengths than random networks.

Scale-Free Properties: Tests if the network follows a power-law degree distribution. Scale-free networks have few highly connected "hubs" and many sparsely connected nodes - typical of social networks.

vs Facebook Typical: Compares our network to typical Facebook network characteristics, helping us understand if this dataset represents a normal Facebook network or has unique properties.
